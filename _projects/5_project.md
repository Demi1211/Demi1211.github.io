If you read *The dax sneezed*, you probably imagine the dax as a living thing; if you read *The dax melted*, you probably don't. This project, with Julian Grove in the Computational Approaches to Linguistic Meaning (CALM) Lab, looks at how people infer whether an unfamiliar referent is animate from the verb it appears with—and especially at the *distribution* of those judgments.

Two verbs can have the same average animacy rating, yet for one verb most people give similar middle-of-the-scale responses, while for another people split into two groups. We first use a felicity-judgment task to estimate these verb-specific distributions with hierarchical Bayesian models, which represent uncertainty and let us compare whether a verb looks gradient or polarized across participants. We then use a Maze reading task, where readers choose between two words at each step, to test whether the shape of the earlier animacy distribution predicts the shape of reading times at a later pronoun (*he*, *she*, or *it*).
