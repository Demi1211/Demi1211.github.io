## University of Florida

### ENC 1102: Argument and Persuasion
_Instructor of Record, Summer B 2026_ &nbsp;<span class="gatoreval"><i class="fa-solid fa-award"></i> GatorEvals recognition</span>

Research-based argument across the humanities, natural sciences, and social sciences. The course has one major assignment per discipline:

- **Humanities:** a comparative analysis of two literary texts (1,000 words).
- **Natural sciences:** a literature review with an annotated bibliography on a natural-science topic.
- **Social sciences:** a short survey that each student designs and runs over five days, keeping a daily logbook of the data collection. The survey becomes a 2,000-word research report with a poster presentation.
- **Public audience:** a final blog post that reworks the research for general readers.

Peer review, writing-process reports, and a closing reflection letter run through the term.

### ENC 3246: Professional Communication for Engineers
_Instructor of Record (online), Summer A 2026_ &nbsp;<span class="gatoreval"><i class="fa-solid fa-award"></i> GatorEvals recognition</span>

Professional writing for engineering majors, taught as a series of project-based modules:

- **Job application packet:** a cover letter and résumé targeted at a real internship or entry-level position, plus an elevator pitch for networking.
- **IEEE-style memo:** a recommendation memo on an engineering scenario, introducing IEEE citation and source types.
- **Failure analysis paper** (1,500 words): a case study of an engineering failure in the student's field, covering its context, an analysis of what went wrong, and recommendations for practice.
- **Group research report:** teams pose a research question and collect original data, and each student writes up the findings.
- **Grant proposal:** teams propose an engineering solution to a funding agency, building on the research report.

### LIN 2008: Language and Computers
_Teaching Assistant (Instructor: Dr. Sarah Moeller), Spring 2026_ &nbsp;<span class="gatoreval"><i class="fa-solid fa-award"></i> GatorEvals recognition</span>

How computers handle human language, organized around one question each week:

- Can computers "read" or "hear" any language? (binary, Unicode, writing systems, speech recognition and synthesis)
- How do spell-checkers and grammar checkers work, and how does language become math? (probability, Naive Bayes)
- Can computers teach languages, hold good conversations, or know what we mean?
- Can language solve crimes? (forensic linguistics)
- Does AI kill languages, and how has translation shaped our view of AI?

Students write a 2,000-word essay on a language-technology issue. I ran the weekly in-person discussion sections, where quizzes and hands-on activities took place.

### LIN 1140: Language and Emotion
_Teaching Assistant / Grader (Instructor: Dr. Eleonora Rossi), Fall 2025_

How people express emotion through face, body, voice, and words, with readings from cognitive neuroscience, experimental linguistics, sociolinguistics, psychology, and literary studies (for example, Darwin, Ekman, de Waal, and Johnson-Laird & Oatley). Across the term, students post examples of emotional expression they find ("data posts") and reflections on them. They write two essays: one analyzing emotion in a story, film scene, speech, or observed conversation, and one on an object they choose at the Harn Museum of Art. Three exams cover the readings.

### ENC 2256: Writing in the Disciplines
_Instructor of Record, Summer B 2025_

Writing for the student's own major or career path:

- **Continuous-learning reports:** four short reports on trade publications, podcasts, or talks in their field.
- **Application documents:** a cover letter and résumé.
- **Industry analysis white paper:** a report on a practice in their field, with an annotated bibliography.
- **Short proposal:** a proposal for improving that practice.
- **Knowledge-sharing project:** a multimodal piece that explains something from their field to the public.
- **Planning memos:** short memos that plan each project, as one would at work.

### ENC 3254: Professional Writing in the Discipline
_Teaching Assistant, Summer A 2024_

Workplace writing for students across majors:

- **Professional profile and job application packet:** a short bio, plus a cover letter and résumé for a real position.
- **Innovation white paper:** an explanation of an innovation in the student's field.
- **Field or research report:** research on a current topic in the student's industry.
- **Capstone:** a recommendation report or proposal for a real audience.

### ENC 1101: Expository and Argumentative Writing
_Instructor of Record, Fall 2023 – Spring 2025: four semesters, eight face-to-face and online sections, 200+ students_

ENC 1101 is UF's first-year writing course. My sections were built around one semester-long project: each student chose a problem in their own community and followed it from first reaction to proposed solution, one assignment at a time.

1. **Personal engagement in a public conversation:** an op-ed, or a call-in statement to a decision-maker, about how the issue affects them, supported by at least one authoritative source.
2. **Issue prospectus:** the questions they want to answer, why a solution matters, their stake in it, and five annotated sources.
3. **Causal analysis** (1,500 words): what causes the problem, or what effects it could have on the community, argued from research.
4. **Evaluation of solutions** (1,500 words): at least two possible solutions compared against criteria the student develops for the context.
5. **Writing self-assessment:** a look back at their own drafts and a plan for improving.
6. **Proposal and presentation** (1,800 words): a feasible solution argued to the key stakeholder, then presented to them.

Along the way, students worked on the rhetorical appeals (ethos, pathos, logos), audience, evaluating sources, and revision. Every paper went through peer review, and students could revise one paper after grading. The textbook was *Everyone's an Author* with *The Little Seagull Handbook*. I taught both in-person (MWF) and asynchronous online sections.

In 2024, with Jian Meng, I gave a talk on personalized approaches to teaching ESL writers at the University Writing Program's *Pedagogy, Practice, and Philosophy* conference.
