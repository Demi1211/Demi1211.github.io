## University of Florida

### ENC 1102: Argument and Persuasion
_Instructor of Record, Summer B 2026_ &nbsp;<span class="gatoreval"><i class="fa-solid fa-award"></i> GatorEvals recognition</span>

Research-based argument across the humanities, natural sciences, and social sciences. The course is built around one assignment per discipline: a comparative analysis of two literary texts; a literature review and annotated bibliography on a natural-science topic; and a five-day survey that students design and run themselves, logged day by day. The survey becomes a 2,000-word research report and poster presentation. A blog post at the end reworks the research for a public audience.

### ENC 3246: Professional Communication for Engineers

Writing for engineering workplaces, taught online as part of the University Writing Program's Writing in the Disciplines courses.

### LIN 2008: Language and Computers
_Teaching Assistant (Instructor: Dr. Sarah Moeller), Spring 2026_ &nbsp;<span class="gatoreval"><i class="fa-solid fa-award"></i> GatorEvals recognition</span>

How computers handle human language, organized around one question a week:

- Can computers "read" or "hear" any language? (text encoding and Unicode, speech recognition and synthesis)
- How do spell-checkers and grammar checkers work, and how does language become math? (probability, Naive Bayes)
- Can computers teach languages, hold good conversations, or know what we mean?
- Can language solve crimes? (forensic linguistics)
- Does AI kill languages?

Students write a 2,000-word essay on a language-technology issue. I ran weekly in-person discussion sections.

### LIN 1140: Language and Emotion
_Teaching Assistant / Grader (Instructor: Dr. Eleonora Rossi), Fall 2025_

How people express emotion through face, body, voice, and words, with readings from cognitive neuroscience, experimental linguistics, sociolinguistics, psychology, and literary studies (for example Darwin, Ekman, de Waal, and Johnson-Laird & Oatley). Students analyze emotional expression in a scene, speech, or story of their choice, and write an experiential-learning essay on an object in the Harn Museum of Art.

### ENC 2256: Writing in the Disciplines

Writing in the genres of students' own fields: analyzing how writing works in a discipline and writing for its readers.
